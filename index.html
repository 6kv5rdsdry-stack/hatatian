A Daughter's Promise

Born from love.
Built for eternity.

Hatatian Jewelry began not as a business, but as a promise. When Hanly Mutsune lost her mother — a woman who wore jewelry the way others wear their hearts — she turned grief into something lasting. She began making jewelry herself, first from brass in local Kenyan markets, eventually working her way toward the rare and the extraordinary.

"My mother loved jewelry. This is my way of continuing that love — through stones that outlive the moment they are given."

Eight years later, Hatatian is a private jewelry house rooted in ethical East African gemstones and built on a single belief: that every piece should carry a story as remarkable as the stone inside it.

not just sellers.

The Black Diamond Collection — Out of Loss, Something Eternal

The Black Diamond Collection includes rings, necklaces, earrings, bangles, and tennis bracelets, including pieces designed specifically for men who want to wear diamonds without softness.

Kenya and East Africa sit atop one of the most geologically remarkable regions on earth. The same ancient forces that shaped the Great Rift Valley also crystallised some of the world's rarest gems. We source them with care, trace them with integrity, and make them available to those who understand what they hold.

A Daughter's Promise

The story
behind Hatatian.

Hatatian Jewelry was not built from a business plan. It was built from grief, and love, and a daughter's need to do something with both of them.

When Hanly Mutsune lost her mother eight years ago, she lost the person in her life who understood jewelry the way most people understand language — as a form of expression, of identity, of love made visible. Her mother wore pieces the way others wear their hearts on their sleeve. She knew what a ring meant on a particular finger. She understood the difference between something worn and something carried.

After her mother passed, Hanly began making jewelry herself. Not as a business at first — as a practice. She started with brass, in Kenyan local markets, learning the craft the same way she learned everything else: by doing it until she understood it. Over eight years, the work deepened. The materials became rarer. The clients became fewer and more serious. The stones became the story.

"My mother loved jewelry. This is my way of continuing that love — through stones that outlive the moment they are given."

Today, Hatatian is a private jewelry house built on ethical East African gemstones. We are based in Mombasa. We work by appointment only — virtually, from anywhere in the world. We take on a limited number of commissions each season because each one deserves full attention.

We specialise in gemstones that Africa has given the world and that the world has not always credited back to Africa. Tsavorite garnet — discovered in Kenya's Taita Taveta region — is 200 times rarer than emerald, and still not a household name. Teal sapphires from Garbatula. Dragon garnets from Tanzania. Spinel, aquamarine, and diamonds from across the continent. Stones with provenance, with character, with a full chain of custody from the hands that found them to yours.

This is not simply a jewelry business. It is a way of honoring the earth that produced these stones, the people who found them, and the woman who first showed a young girl that what you wear can be an act of love.
